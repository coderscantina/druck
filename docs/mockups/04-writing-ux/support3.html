A tariff for every load

By 1620 the tariff ran to eleven classes, from fine table salt in barrels to the coarse Fuder sold to tanners. The steepest rate fell on salt leaving by the Pass Lueg road, twelve Kreuzer the Zentner against eight on the Salzach barges.

A horse with two sacks still paid its four Kreuzer at Golling, as Table 5.1 sets out, but the archbishop now took a share of every one.

Interview with Anna Salzmann
research/interview-salzmann.md · Hallein, 14 March 2024
Cited twice here: The bridge at Golling, Smuggling

How much did a collector actually take?

Less than people think, and more than the archbishop knew. The wage was a pittance. But look at the strokes on the boards. They did not steal from the archbishop. They rounded, and the rounding always went their way.

Is that in the books?

Only as a pattern. Every load at Golling between 1608 and 1615 ends on a whole Zentner.
