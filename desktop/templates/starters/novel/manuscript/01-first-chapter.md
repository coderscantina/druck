# The first chapter

The ferry left at six, and Anna was the only passenger who stayed on deck. Behind her the harbour lights went out one by one, as if someone were walking through the town and closing doors. She had a suitcase, a borrowed coat, and a letter she had read so often that the folds had started to tear.

The crossing took two hours. A man in the wheelhouse raised his hand once and did not look at her again. The sea was flat and grey, the colour of a slate roof after rain, and the island came up out of it so slowly that she could not have said when she first saw it.

"You will be met," the letter said. It did not say by whom.

Nobody met her. The door was open, the stove was warm, and on the kitchen table lay a second letter in the same hand as the first. She sat down in her coat and read it twice before the light went.[^lamp]

The second letter was shorter. It named a room at the top of the stairs, a drawer in that room, and a key that she would find under the third stone of the garden wall. It asked her to wait until morning. She did not wait.
